# ML / AI review — picking the right side, faster

2026-09-27. A code review of the on-device models, the blend in `ScoringEngine`, the side picker, and the offline trainers. It is checked against the 2026-09-25 backtest (`docs/backtest-2026-09-25.md`).

## What the backtest says

| Forecast (103,597 decision minutes) | Brier | Log-loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Market mid** | **0.1592** | **0.4757** |
| Digital fair Φ(d2) | 0.1611 | 0.4825 |
| Blended fair (what the app shows) | 0.1610 | 0.4841 |
| Fallback MLP ("AI") | 0.1645 | 0.4961 |

**No model beats the Kalshi mid.** The blend makes the market's probability slightly worse. The app's pick wins 65% of the time out of sample, but it buys at a 65.8¢ average and loses 3.3% ROI. It is picking the favorite, and the favorite is already priced right. Favorite win rate tracks price within about 1pp in every bucket.

"The correct side" has to mean **the side whose win chance is above its ask plus fee**, not the side more likely to win. Win rate is the wrong target. Until a model beats the mid on out-of-sample Brier, no side it picks has an edge.

## Findings, most important first

### 1. Weekly edge model trained on the answer — **fixed in this PR**

`ml/train_edge.py` fetched Coinbase spot from `open − 15m` up to the **market close**, then used `spot[-1]` (the settlement-minute price) for every row. That included the mid-window rows. So `dist_to_strike_vol` and `digital_fair` were built from the price that decides the result. Realized vol also used future bars.

What that caused:

- Walk-forward Brier looked far better than the market, so `beats_market` came out `true`.
- `EdgeModelManifest` gates activation on `beats_market`. The app then downloaded a model that had learned "digital_fair ≈ outcome". In live use it gets the *current* spot, so it is badly overconfident. It still moved the blended fair (0.45 × its 0.35 market blend, about 16% net weight), and its `qualifiesEdge` gated alerts.

The fix: `fetch_spot` now keeps the bar timestamps. `spot_known_at()` keeps only 1m bars that have closed by the decision candle, and only the last 16 of them (the same window the app uses for realized vol). `cross_asset` is now the pre-decision 5m spot return. On the phone this feature is `spotReturn5m`, but training had it hard-coded to 0. `ml/test_train_edge.py` checks that a row's features don't change when only the settlement-minute spot changes.

**Action:** run *Actions → Train edge model* again, and treat the current `edge-model-latest` release as invalid. After retraining, expect `beats_market: false`. That result is honest, and it is why items 2–4 matter.

### 2. Model the market's error, not the outcome

Every channel predicts P(YES) from scratch, then gets averaged with the mid. Train the model **with the market as a fixed offset** instead:

```
logit P(YES) = logit(mid) + β·x        (x = spot-vs-strike, spot momentum, flow, …)
```

With β = 0 it *is* the market, so it can't do worse than the mid by construction. Any nonzero β has to earn its place on held-out data. In `train_edge.py` this means adding `logit(mid)` as an offset in `fit_logistic`, not as a standardized feature. The app side would apply `sigmoid(logit(mid) + w·z)` instead of `blendWithMarket`. Then pick the side by expected value at the **ask**:

```
ev_yes = p − yes_ask − fee(yes_ask)
ev_no  = (1 − p) − no_ask − fee(no_ask)
side   = argmax(ev_yes, ev_no), or skip if both ≤ margin
```

This replaces the current order of `DirectionSanity` → `TapeConflict.primaryFromMarket` → `resolveSide`. Today spot-vs-strike forces the side (`TicketBuilder.resolveSide` prefers `primaryHeroSide`). That order guarantees the favorite gets bought whatever the price.

### 3. "Related crypto" channel is a bug (weight 0.08 early / 0.04 late)

`TickBook.relatedCryptoMid` returns the *other* coins' YES mid, and `ScoringEngine` blends it in as if it were *this* market's P(YES). BTC and ETH have different strikes, so ETH at 30¢ says nothing about whether BTC finishes above its strike. With BTC at 90¢ and ETH at 30¢, the channel alone pulls fair down about 5pp and makes a fake NO edge. The backtest pipeline has the same bug (`pipeline.py` `related_pp`).

Proposed fix (in both `ScoringEngine` and `pipeline.py`, keeping parity): use the related coins' recent **change** in mid or spot return as a signed nudge, `midPp + k·tanh(Δrelated / s)`. Or drop the channel until it is fitted (item 5). I have not changed it here: the Kotlin parity test can't run in this container (no Android SDK download), and the change needs the backtest re-run to confirm.

### 4. The "AI" MLP has the largest weight and the least information

`diphunter.tflite` / `FallbackWeights` (`W_AI` = 0.30 early, 0.20 late):

- It was trained on **1,120 rows**, from BTC plus **WTI oil** (`series_id` 0/1). It has never seen ETH or SOL, and all crypto maps to id 0.
- All 8 inputs come from the Kalshi mid (mid, 0.5−mid, momentum, vol of mid, volume, OI, TTE). There is no spot, so it can only re-describe the price it is trying to beat.
- In the backtest it has the worst Brier of any forecast.

Recommendation: set `W_AI`/`W_AI_LATE` to 0 until it is retrained. Retrain it on the backtest's ~100k decision minutes with spot features, using the offset setup from item 2, and remove WTI.

### 5. Hand-set blend weights and slopes → fit them

All nine channels have hand-set constants: `8·tanh(flow)`, `6·tanh(vel/2)`, `8·imb`, `7·cancel`, `6·tanh(ret/0.003)`, plus the regime multipliers. None of them were fitted to data. Each channel is already a deviation from the mid (`featureDevs`), so they drop straight into item 2 as `x`. One walk-forward logistic regression on the backtest dataset gives the weights, and channels whose coefficient CI includes 0 get dropped. Only the channels the backtest can rebuild (velocity, flow sign, spot, digital fair) can be fitted now. Book, cancel and lead-lag need live recordings (item 8).

### 6. Faster: stream spot, don't poll it

The side on these contracts is decided by spot vs strike. Spot is currently the slowest input:

- `ExternalMarketFeatures` polls Coinbase/Binance REST with a **25s cache** (`EXTERNAL_CACHE_MS`), and uses 1m candles.
- `ret1m` compares the in-progress 1m candle with the previous close, so its horizon wanders between 0 and 60s.

When spot crosses the strike, Kalshi quotes take a few seconds to reprice. That gap is the only edge a phone can realistically capture. Recommendations:

1. Subscribe to the Coinbase Exchange WebSocket `ticker` channel for BTC/ETH/SOL-USD, and keep a ring buffer of the last ~20 minutes of trades.
2. Recompute `DigitalOptionFairValue` on **every spot tick**, not every Kalshi tick. Fire when `p_digital − ask − fee` clears the margin.
3. Use an EWMA of 10s returns over ~60 minutes for σ. `realizedVol(closes.takeLast(16))` is 15 samples and very noisy, and σ is the digital model's only parameter.
4. Kalshi settles on a benchmark index, not the Coinbase last trade. Check the contract rules for the exact index and any averaging window in the final minute. Model that window in the last 60–120s instead of using the point spot. The backtest's 95.5% Coinbase match suggests the proxy is close but not exact.

### 7. Earlier isn't better: the first-minute entry is where it loses

Out-of-sample, app pick, by time left:

| Time left | Bets | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|
| 14–12m | 995 | **−4.5%** |
| 11–9m | 187 | +3.3% |
| 8–6m | 124 | −3.8% |

By spot distance from strike:

| Spot − strike | Bets | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|
| < 5bp | 623 | **−7.7%** |
| 5–20bp | 693 | +0.3% |

Two-thirds of bets fire in the first 3 minutes, and those carry most of the loss. Near-strike markets are coin flips, and paying 57¢ for them loses the spread and the fee. Neither slice has a CI that excludes zero, so treat these as hypotheses, not rules. The practical meaning of "faster" is **reacting faster to spot** (item 6), not entering earlier in the window. A cheap guard until the model beats the market: skip when |spot − strike| < ~5bp unless the EV at the ask is well above the fee.

**Status (`Claude` branch): added as a configurable guard, on by default.** `EntryFilter` blocks entry while fewer than 3 of the 15 minutes have elapsed (nothing before 12:00 left; 12:00 itself passes). It also blocks when |spot − strike| < 5bp, unless the picked side's net edge is ≥ 10pp. Missing spot or strike never blocks. It gates alerts (through `passedFilter`), auto tickets and paper auto-fills (even with "Require skip filter…" off), and the home BET call, which shows the reason. Buy anyway still works. Thresholds are in Settings → Entry filter. The backtest applies the same rule (`pipeline.entry_filter`): `app_entry` at the defaults, plus an IS-tuned sweep (0/2/3/5 min × 0/3/5/10bp) reported OOS. Caveat: the defaults came from this OOS breakdown, so on those days only the IS-tuned row is independent evidence. Days after 2026-09-25 are the real test.

### 8. The Heavy ML / extended AI stack is untrained

Shipped state of the stack (CNN, LSTM, GBM, multi-task heads, RL sizer, news, conformal, meta-labeler, path sim):

- It runs on compiled Kotlin priors. The asset JSON says "Empty trees keep the compiled Kotlin GBM prior".
- `train_heavy.py` needs a CSV of 10s sequences (size / imbalance / aggressor) that nobody has recorded.
- It is default off, which is right, and it can't be backtested because there is no historical L2 or taker side.

Recommendation: keep it off. Start **logging the live WebSocket stream** (book deltas, trades with taker side, spot ticks) to the local archive or Supabase. After a few weeks there is a real training set, and the book-based channels can be fitted and tested honestly. More model families on no data won't improve accuracy.

### 9. Online adapter learns from too few settlements

`OnlineAdapter` starts reweighting channels after `MIN_ADAPTER_SAMPLES = 8` settlements, with learning rate 0.08. At a Brier of about 0.16, 8 outcomes can't separate a good channel from noise, and the weights drift toward whatever won last. Raise the gate to ≥ 100 settlements, and shrink toward the fitted prior (for example, an L2 pull to weight 1.0).

## Suggested order

1. ✅ Fix the trainer look-ahead (this PR). Re-run the workflow and invalidate the old release.
2. ✅ (`Claude` branch) `W_AI` and `W_RELATED` set to 0 in `ScoringEngine` and `pipeline.py`. Cross-coin moves stay covered by lead–lag. Still to do: re-run the backtest on the cached data.
3. Refit the trainer with the market logit as an offset, train on all ~100k backtest minutes (not 2 per market), and choose the side by EV at the ask.
4. Stream spot over WebSocket, use EWMA σ, and model the settlement-index averaging window.
5. Record the live book/trade/spot stream. Revisit Heavy ML after about 4 weeks of data.
6. ✅ (`Claude` branch) Entry filter guard (item 7): no entry in the first 3 minutes or within 5bp of the strike (unless net edge ≥ 10pp). Still to do: re-run the backtest and read the `app_entry` / IS-tuned entry rows before changing the defaults.

Success criterion for each step: out-of-sample Brier and log-loss **below the market mid**, and a $/bet bootstrap CI that excludes zero at the *close ask* fill. A higher hit rate doesn't count on its own.
