## Concrete V2 fix

V2 profit-trail arming and the final-minute profitable-hold check now use the trade's recorded entry fee. Before this change they recomputed one fee at the average entry price. The simulator rounds its entry fee at each walked book level, so ten one-contract depth fills can cost 20 cents while the average-price estimate gives 17 cents. At a 40c entry and 47.5c bid, the old calculation arms a 4c net trail; the recorded-fee calculation yields only 3.7c/contract and correctly waits. The close-time profitability check uses the same accounting. Existing protective-stop thresholds and latched exits remain in force.

This corrects the simulator's internal fee accounting; it does not claim the per-level fee approximation exactly matches an exchange's cross-fill accumulator. Exit fees used for arming remain a single-bid estimate. Actual subsequent fills still charge the simulator's displayed-depth and partial-fill fees. The change has no measured profit uplift without a quote replay.

## Testable route to higher paper returns

First use `tools/scalp_lab/analyze.py` on an app database backup or raw scalp-row export. It validates net against proceeds minus both fees and entry cost, isolates completed windows across a chronological day split, preserves exact variants and adaptive cohorts, and ranks entry-cap experiments using the fitting period only. A small locked cap grid tests whether avoiding expensive entries improves later recorded returns. All-time dollars or a high win rate alone are insufficient: the recorded 78%-winning maker strategy still lost 1.50c/contract.

Candidates for further paper experiments should have positive net cents/contract in a genuinely new forward period, spread across multiple days/windows, and retain that advantage with later-book fills and both fees. Focus on measured CF-to-book repricing or a calibrated short-horizon forecast that clears spread and both fees. Moving a take-profit or stop alone does not supply predictive drift. Scale only after depth replay shows that the additional contracts keep positive marginal returns; a 25-contract walk can erase a small edge.

No new profitable model is asserted here. The ledger analyzer provides the missing readout needed to choose a model and its next experiment from actual observations.
