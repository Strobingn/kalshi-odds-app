# Scalping KXBTC15M — rules, then ML — 2026-10-09

Question from Dirk: scalping is the only way to get an edge on Kalshi; get the
AI / ML to find it.

**Answer, updated the same afternoon with real order books (sections G–I):
none of the scalps tested here makes money for an order that joins the best
price.** With the size really shown ahead counted, the shipped model's picks
lost 1.6¢ per order on all 7 recorded days, a model given the book and
one-second Bitcoin chose not to trade, and the "fewer than about 250 ahead"
rule below was wrong: a short queue is a level about to break. The earlier
answer (sections A–F, trade prints only) was that a scalp pays only from the
front of the queue, a fraction of a cent; that part stands as a statement
about a place in line no joining order gets. Nothing here was traded. Not
financial advice.

Scalp = open and close inside the same 15-minute window. Designs were written
down before scoring (`tools/research/scalp/DESIGN.md`). Intervals resample
whole UTC days.

## Costs

Taker fee `0.07 × P × (1 − P)` on the way in and again on the way out, plus a
1¢ spread: 4.5¢ per contract round trip at 40–60¢, 3.9¢ at 20–40¢ / 60–80¢,
2.5¢ nearer the ends. Resting orders pay no fee on this series.

## A. Minute-level scalps (4,264 windows, 2026-08-24 → 10-08)

Buy at the ask at minutes 2–11, sell at the bid 1–3 minutes later. Nine
signals (always the favourite, spot momentum 1 and 3 min, Kalshi momentum,
Kalshi fade, spot-model lag 3¢ / 5¢, spot jump follow / fade) × three holds.
In sample to 09-22, out of sample 09-23 → 10-08.

- All 27 cells lose out of sample: −1.6¢ to −4.3¢ per contract.
- Best in sample (spot-model lag ≥ 5¢, hold 3 min): **−3.23¢ out of sample**,
  95% [−4.14, −2.28], 3,935 trades.
- The same 39,074 favourite entries, two exits: held to settlement **−0.78¢**
  [−1.77, +0.15]; sold a minute later **−3.15¢** [−3.26, −3.02]. Selling early
  costs about 2.4¢ more than holding.

## B. Second-level scalps (1,992 windows, every public trade, 2026-09-18 → 10-09)

Quotes rebuilt from the tape each second. Decisions every 5 s. A resting order
fills either only when a print trades *through* its price (back of the queue)
or on any print *at* its price (front of the queue). In sample 09-18 → 10-02,
out of sample 10-03 → 10-09. Signals: none, 10-second momentum, 10-second
fade, 30-second taker flow, flow fade.

| Family | Cells | Out of sample, ¢ per scalp | Pre-registered pick, out of sample |
|---|---:|---|---|
| B1 buy at the ask, sell at the bid (target 2/4¢, stop 2/4¢, 30/120 s) | 40 | −3.92 to −3.45, none positive | −3.54, 99% [−3.67, −3.38], 0 of 7 days |
| B2 rest in, rest out +1/+2¢ (stop 2/4¢, 30/120 s), back of queue | 40 | −3.07 to −2.85, none positive | −2.86, 99% [−2.97, −2.73], 0 of 7 days |
| B2, front of queue (context) | 40 | −0.76 to +0.38, 16 positive | no signal, +1¢, stop 4¢: +0.18 [+0.07, +0.30] |
| B3 rest in, hold to settlement, back of queue | 5 | −3.60 to +0.51 | flow fade: +0.51, 99% [−3.55, +3.52] |
| B3, front of queue (context) | 5 | −2.27 to +3.15 | no signal: +0.38 [+0.33, +0.45] |

A bid joined at the best bid fills within 20 s 74% of the time at the back of
the queue and 98% at the front: the price trades through the bid most of the
time, which is why back-of-queue fills are bad ones.

Limit: scalps with a 120 s limit entered after about 12:40 that were still open
at the close were dropped, not settled (at most 4 of 145 decision times). The
picks above are 30 s cells or have a 30 s twin within 0.01¢.

time left, price moves over 5–300 s, volatility, taker flow and volume over

## E. The model that ships in the app, and the fast strategies

**Compact model** (`train_compact.py` → `assets/scalper_model.json`). The app
can rebuild 23 of the features from live trade prints with a 60-second
lookback, so the shipped model uses only those. Same protocol. Threshold =
the validation cut with the highest total P&L (top 70%, "take everything the
model expects to pay"). Test days: its picks made **+0.35¢** per order at the
front of the queue, 99% [+0.24, +0.47], 7 of 7 days, against +0.18¢ for every
order; −2.02¢ at the back of the queue. `ScalperParityTest` replays a
recorded window in the app's code and checks all 23 features and every model
output against the Python values.

**Queue penalty** (`export_app_model.py`). Fitted on the first three test
days, the picks lose 0.23¢ with 100 contracts ahead, 0.46¢ at 250, 0.74¢ at
500, 1.13¢ at 1,000, 1.60¢ at 2,000. Taking only orders whose prediction
minus that penalty is at least zero, on the other four test days:
+0.16¢ per order at 100 ahead (30,872 orders) and +0.16¢ at 250 ahead
(12,051 orders), where taking every pick made 0.00¢ and −0.26¢. Past about
500 ahead that rule takes nothing.

**Fast strategies** (`strat_bt.py`, all 22 days, rules fixed before scoring):
dip-hunter (the side fell 2¢ or more in 10 s), momentum-sniper (rose 2¢ or
more in 10 s), extreme reversion (fell 6¢ or more in 30 s).

| Strategy | Buy at the ask, offer +2/+2/+3¢, stop 2/2/3¢, 30/30/60 s | Rest a bid, offer +1¢, stop 4¢, 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| Dip-hunter | −3.68¢ back of queue, −3.27¢ front, 0 of 22 days | −2.25¢ back, −0.00¢ front |
| Momentum-sniper | −3.49¢, −3.07¢, 0 of 22 days | −2.13¢ back, +0.06¢ front [+0.02, +0.11] |
| Extreme reversion | −3.67¢, −3.26¢, 0 of 22 days | −2.25¢ back, −0.01¢ front |

Each signal fires in about 40–60 of the 145 five-second slots per window and
side. Paying the ask on them lost on every one of the 22 days.

## F. In the app (1.8.4): the paper scalper

Home card **Paper scalper · aggressive · PAPER ONLY**. Seven strategies run
at once on the live Bitcoin window, 10 contracts an order, both sides, several
positions each, no caps: the ML scalper (resting), and dip-hunter,
momentum-sniper and extreme reversion each in a buy-now and a resting form.
The fast ones decide every 2 s, the ML scalper every 5 s as trained.

- Filled by real public trade prints. A resting order fills when a print
  trades through its price or when the queue ahead plus its own size has
  traded at its price. The queue is the size the Live signals book shows at
  that price (3,500 assumed when the book is not on). Nobody ahead cancels.
- Buy-now entries and stop / time-out exits pay the exact taker fee.
- The scoreboard shows every strategy with its history beside it, and for the
  ML scalper the split by queue (0–100, 101–250, 251–1,000, over 1,000) and
  the orders the queue penalty says should pay.
- It has no trade client and no credentials. Settings → Paper scalper turns
  it off and resets it.

What to expect from history: the buy-now rows lose about 3¢ a contract, the
resting rows sit near zero, and the ML scalper is positive only in the
0–100 and (queue-aware) 101–250 rows. The record on the phone is the first
test of any of it against real displayed queues.

## G–I. Real order books (added 2026-10-09, afternoon)

The cloud recorder's files were copied out of the Actions cache (`export-recordings.yml`, release
`scalper-data`): one snapshot a second of the live window's best bid and ask **with the size shown**, and
Coinbase BTC-USD about once a second, 2026-10-03 → 10-09. Joined with the complete trade tape that is 577
windows on 7 days, which are the test days of the shipped model. Design fixed in
`tools/research/scalp/DESIGN.md` ("Real order books") before any of these numbers; simulator `rec_sim.py`
(31 hand-built checks in `test_rec_sim.py`).

Order scored: 10 contracts joined at the book's best bid, 20 s to fill, then an offer 1¢ higher, stop 4¢,
time-out 120 s, taker fee rounded up per order on every sale at the bid. "Book" fill rule: the size shown
ahead when the order is posted must trade, and the contracts ahead can never be more than the size shown at
that price in any later snapshot. Cents per order sent (unfilled = 0), 95% interval resampling the 7 days.

**G. The shipped model and rules, nothing fitted** (`rec_g.py`, 120,158 rows every 5 s)

| | first in line | real queue ("book") | last in line |
|---|---|---|---|
| every row | −0.07¢ | **−1.80¢** [−1.92, −1.66], 0 of 7 days | −2.23¢ |
| shipped model's picks (76% of rows) | +0.18¢ | **−1.61¢** [−1.73, −1.48], 0 of 7 | −2.06¢ |
| its queue-aware picks (2,091 rows) | −0.76¢ | **−1.71¢** [−1.99, −1.51], 0 of 7 | −2.25¢ |

- The size at the best bid: median 4,423 contracts; 250 or fewer on 4.5% of rows.
- **A short queue is the worst place, not the best.** With 0–100 ahead: −2.15¢, and −1.35¢ even first in
  line. With over 3,000 ahead: −1.77¢, +0.12¢ first in line. A thin best bid is a level about to break, so
  the queue penalty built from the trade tape pointed the "queue-aware" rule at the wrong orders.
- 81% of joined bids fill inside 20 s. They are hit when the price is falling through them.
- Posting one tick better when the spread is 2¢ (906 rows): −1.63¢ [−1.83, −1.46]. Joining and holding to
  settlement: −0.89¢ [−1.07, −0.74].
- Fast signals, 0 of 7 days each: dip −2.05¢ resting / −3.86¢ buying now; momentum −1.74¢ / −3.29¢; extreme
  −2.07¢ / −3.88¢.

**H. A model that sees the book and Bitcoin** (`rec_h.py`, 313,616 rows every 2 s, leave one day out)

50 inputs: the shipped 23, 14 from the book (sizes, imbalance, microprice, age and direction of the best
bid, book mid moves) and 13 from one-second Coinbase (returns over 1–60 s, distance from the start price,
fair value minus the book mid and its lead over 5 and 10 s). In all 7 folds the rule chosen on the inner
validation day was **do not trade**. Out of fold the model ranks orders (worst tenth −3.35¢, best tenth
−0.83¢) but its best 1% still averages −0.74¢ and its best 0.1% −0.48¢. Book inputs help a little (best
1% −0.51¢ with them, −0.72¢ without); the Coinbase inputs do not (−0.79¢). The recorder's Coinbase price
trails Kalshi's quotes by about a second (`lag_study.py`), so it cannot warn of anything.

**I. The same inputs on the buying-at-the-ask side**

What the takers in the tape made (`rec_markout.py`, 705 million contracts at 10–90¢): 10 s after a trade
the book mid is 0.06¢ against the price they paid; held to settlement −0.14¢ [−0.40, +0.13]; the fee
averages 1.39¢; net **−1.53¢** [−1.79, −1.27]. Orders under 100 contracts lose 0.59¢ before the fee,
orders of 1,000 or more make +0.28¢ [−0.28, +0.87]. So the resting side as a whole earns about a tenth
of a cent a contract: it collects from small orders and hands most of it to large ones.

The first run of the taker model scored +1.9¢ for "buy at the ask, sell at the bid 10 s later". That
was a flaw in the test, found before anything was built on it: a snapshot can be up to a second older
than the trade prints of the same second, so the model was buying at a price that had already gone.
With the buy meeting the next snapshot instead, nothing is left on any of the six targets. Selling after
5 s or 10 s, the 2¢ target / 2¢ stop, and a limit at the price seen: the model picks nothing (best 0.1%
of predictions −1.61¢, −1.85¢, −1.37¢, −0.15¢). Selling after 30 s or 60 s: it picks about 1% of rows in
a few folds and they lose 2.85¢ and 3.63¢. The flawed number is still a measurement of something real: acting inside the second,
before the quote moves, was worth about 2¢ a contract. That is the trade the fast accounts make.

**What G–I change.** Section E's "+0.35¢ first in line" does not survive a real book: priced at the
book's bid it is +0.18¢, and nobody joining a 4,000-contract level is first in line. Every rule the paper
scalper runs lost on all 7 days once the real queue was counted. The card's figures in brackets now show
these numbers.

## What this means

*(Written before sections G–I; the first two points stand, the third is now tested and fails for an order
that joins the best bid.)*



- A hand-approved order cannot scalp this market. By the time Approve is
  tapped the order is behind the queue, where every version loses.
- The only scalp with an edge is a resting one near the front, at about
  +0.2 to +0.4¢ per contract. At the app's $5 order (about 10 contracts) that
  is 2–4¢ a scalp.
- Getting near the front means posting the moment a price level opens and
  cancelling when the queue ahead grows or the model's calm reading ends.
  That is an automatic quoting loop, which the app does not have.

## Not done

- Orders that wait at a price before it becomes the best one. A resting order moves up as the orders
  ahead of it are cancelled and re-entered, and being early is the only way a slow account gets near the
  front. The recordings had only the best level, so this could not be followed. Since 2026-10-09 the
  recorder keeps the 15 best levels a side (`depth_DAY.csv.gz`) and every trade; a few days of that are
  needed first.
- The phone's own data. The cloud snapshots are a second apart and its Coinbase price is slow. The app's
  websocket book and price are faster; 1.8.5 writes every paper order with what it saw.
- The tails (under 10¢, over 90¢), where the tick is a tenth of a cent and the fee is a third of a cent.
- One model family, no tuning. Seven days of books.
